# [Product Name]
## Comprehensive Product Overview & Feature Description — Version 1.0

> **Template adapted from the 22-topic structure used for Repository-to-Course.** Replace prompts with agreed product descriptions. Combine/remove sections only with clear coverage; illustrative text is not a decision. Do not paste the original project's requirements into a new project.

### 1. Product Overview and Vision
What problem exists? For whom? What transformation or outcome should the product create? Why now?

### 2. Product Creation and Operating Model
Who creates, operates, curates, approves or contributes? What responsibilities are inside vs outside the product?

### 3. Core Product Discovery and Entry Experience
How do users discover and understand the product? What should they know before starting?

### 4. Product Structure and Information Organization
How are major concepts, entities, content, and experiences organized from a user's perspective?

### 5. Primary End-to-End User Journey
Describe the main progression from entry to valuable outcome, including necessary stages and decisions.

### 6. User Experience and Content/Interaction Formats
Which formats and modes are essential, appropriate or optional? Define language/accessibility needs where relevant.

### 7. Core Domain Exploration and Transparency
What underlying objects, source materials, workflows or evidence must users inspect? How should they be connected?

### 8. Personalization and Different User Needs
What varies by user goals, knowledge, preferences or context? What is predetermined or adaptive?

### 9. Product Principles and User Independence
Describe the governing experience philosophy and how users gain agency or competence.

### 10. Actions, Tasks and Interactive Behavior
What can users actually do? What completes a meaningful activity? Describe externally visible outcomes.

### 11. Assessment, Feedback and Success Evidence
How does the product distinguish usage/completion from real success? What can be verified and what remains self-reported?

### 12. Accounts, User State and Progress
What is public, authenticated, saved, resumed, private or attributable to a user?

### 13. Quality, Limitations and Trade-offs
How are quality problems, known limitations, alternatives and uncertainty communicated?

### 14. Trust, Accuracy, Review and Approval
What facts require evidence? Who reviews and authorizes consequential publication or changes?

### 15. Versions, Change and Compatibility
What is pinned or stable? How do product/content changes affect existing users and records?

### 16. Portability, Downloads and Independent Use
What can users take away, export or use without the live product, if relevant?

### 17. Access and Permissions
What can anonymous, authenticated, privileged or external actors access and perform?

### 18. Initial Scope, Content or Portfolio Selection
What must exist at first launch? What can be optional? How should selections be justified?

### 19. Validation and Success Criteria
How will the product be tested with real users? Which dimensions matter? What needs approval before formal validation?

### 20. Long-Term Vision and Future Expansion
What valuable ideas are intentionally deferred? Label hypotheses as hypotheses.

### 21. Version 1 Scope and Explicit Limitations
Summarize committed V1 capabilities and clear exclusions. Do not slip future ambitions into V1.

### 22. Overall Product Direction
Connect the problem, core experience, V1 destination and longer-term direction in a coherent statement.

---

## Quality and approval

- Answer relevant questions with **product behavior and value**, not prescribed frameworks, database tables, API design, infrastructure or task breakdowns.
- Check internal consistency, complete V1 coverage, actor/access boundaries, validation and explicit exclusions.
- Replace all prompts/placeholders with decisions or clearly named open questions.
- Present the completed overview for human approval before treating it as the roadmap's authority.
- Save as `docs/product/product-overview-v1.md` (adapt path if target repo already has a documented convention).
